04 Accessibility · Multimodal · Search UX 2021

TATA Motors — Simplifying spare parts search for 52,000 mechanics

Designed a multimodal search experience that helped auto mechanics across India find the right parts faster — using voice, image, and text in their native language.

Role
Product Designer
Timeline

The problem

A 12% revenue decline caused by a search box that didn't work

Tata Genuine Parts was losing 12% of its motor parts revenue — not because of logistics or pricing, but because mechanics couldn't find the parts they needed. Over 52,000 mechanics across India were spending more time searching than repairing.

Research

Six mechanic interviews. One clear truth.

Conducted field interviews with mechanics in Bangalore, Delhi, and Gujarat — sessions held in the mechanic's native language, including Gujarati, to eliminate translation barriers and get to the real experience.

Original design problems

Four critical flaws in the existing experience

One-dimensional search
Limited to a single search method — no voice, no image, no alternatives for mechanics who work with their hands.
Poor visual hierarchy
Disorganized information presentation with no clear path from search to the correct part in the right quantity.
Non-inclusive design
No accessibility considerations for mechanics with varying literacy levels, physical constraints, or low-light garage conditions.
No multilingual support
India has 22 official languages and hundreds of regional dialects. The app only spoke one.
Design solutions

Four ways to search. One experience.

01
Search by Click
Smart defaults on open — shows recent searches, registered vehicle options, and recently viewed parts. One tap to add to cart. Reduces the blank-screen problem that kills momentum.
02
Search by Name
Intelligent suggestions that group results by assembly and kit — not just keyword matches. Highlights last-ordered items first so repeat jobs are instant.
03
Search by Voice
Mechanics speak the part name in their preferred language — Hindi, Gujarati, Tamil, or English. The system understands regional terminology variants like "patti" and "chakka" without needing standardized input.
04
Search by Image
OCR-powered visual identification — mechanics photograph a worn or unfamiliar part to find the right replacement. Dramatically improves accessibility for low-literacy users and complex part identification.
Key learnings

What the first design got wrong

The initial approach — searching by fleet number — failed immediately. It was designed around what the database knew, not what the mechanic experienced. The moment we ran interviews, we found the real mental model: parts have names, and those names change by region.

The biggest shift: stop designing for the system and start designing for the person holding the wrench.
